03 · DOCUMENT CHECKLISTS

NTN registration or annual return?

Choose the service first, then open the categories that apply to you. This is a preparation checklist: supporting records are not all mandatory uploads to IRIS.

1. Individual: salaried, retired or other personal income
  • Valid CNIC copy (front and back); NICOP or passport details where applicable.
  • Mobile SIM in your own CNIC and access to your personal email for verification. Keep control of your OTPs.
  • Full residential address, nationality and income source; employer name and NTN if employed, or property address if earning rent.
  • Bank account maintenance certificate in your own name. Prepare readable PDF scans.
  • Salary slip or pension proof may help confirm your profile; it is supporting information, not a universal NTN enrollment requirement.
2. Sole proprietor, freelancer or business individual
  • All individual documents above. A sole proprietor registers as an individual, with business particulars.
  • Business or trading name, start date, principal activity, complete business address and contact details.
  • Proof of business premises ownership or tenancy, plus a paid business utility bill issued within the last 3 months.
  • Personal bank maintenance certificate; relevant business account details if maintained. Home-based or shared premises cases should be checked individually.
  • No fixed Rs. 200 stamp-paper requirement is assumed here. Any additional evidence depends on your actual premises and registration case.
3. Partnership / Association of Persons (AOP)
  • Partnership deed; Registrar of Firms registration certificate for a registered firm; CNICs of partners or members and their ownership shares.
  • Authorization on AOP letterhead signed by members or partners, identifying the authorized member or representative.
  • AOP email, authorized person’s CNIC-linked mobile, bank maintenance certificate in the AOP name, and premises ownership/tenancy evidence with recent paid utility bill.
  • Business name, activity, address and accounting period. Keep originals ready for the applicable FBR facilitation-counter process.
4. Company, trust or other entity
  • Company incorporation certificate; directors’ identity documents and director/major shareholder details and shares.
  • Company authorization letter identifying the principal officer, company email, officer contact and identity details.
  • Company bank maintenance certificate, premises evidence and paid utility bill within 3 months; business activity, registered address and accounting period.
  • Memorandum/articles or other entity records may be needed for case review. Trusts need their trust deed; societies need their registration certificate.
  • SECP-incorporated entities may already have an NTN. Check existing registration before creating another application; foreign entities require a separate assessment.

Sources: FBR registration particulars and registration process. Requirements vary by taxpayer and enrollment route.

Income and expenses: 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026. Closing assets and debts: 30 June 2026. Supply only applicable categories, and clearly mark anything unavailable.

1. Everyone: identity, tax history and bank records
  • CNIC/NTN and current contact details; existing IRIS registration status. Arrange authorized access securely, not through a public form.
  • Previous return, wealth statement and reconciliation; prior notices, outstanding demands, refunds and tax payments, if any. First-time filers should prepare opening assets and funding history.
  • Statements for every relevant bank account for 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026, including closed accounts used during the year, foreign accounts where relevant, and account balances at 30 June.
  • Tax deduction certificates and CPR/payment receipts: salary, bank profit, contracts, utilities/telecom, vehicle/property transactions and other relevant withholding. A bill alone does not establish that every charge is adjustable.
2. Salaried and retired persons
  • Annual employer salary and tax certificate, salary slips, bonuses, allowances, benefits and employer details; include all employers during the year.
  • Pension certificates/statements, retirement benefits, gratuity or provident fund receipts and applicable tax deduction details. Treatment depends on the payment and facts.
3. Business owners, traders and professionals
  • Annual sales/service receipts, invoices, purchases, returns, expense ledgers and supporting bills, including rent, salaries, utilities, transport and finance costs.
  • Opening and closing stock, receivables/payables, cash, business bank accounts, loans and capital introduced or withdrawn.
  • Fixed-asset register: machinery/equipment cost, purchase/disposal dates and amounts, previous depreciation and business use.
  • Profit and loss account and balance sheet where applicable; withholding certificates, advance tax and relevant sales tax records. Partnership members should supply their share/profit records; entities need entity accounts.
4. Freelancers, exporters and overseas income
  • Client invoices, contracts, platform earnings/fee reports and payment-provider statements, with corresponding bank credits.
  • PRCs/e-PRCs and bank remittance purpose records for foreign receipts; PSEB or other relevant registrations if claiming applicable treatment.
  • Separate export/service earnings from gifts, family remittances, loans and transfers between your own accounts. Foreign receipts are not automatically exempt.
  • Residence/travel dates, passport travel records, foreign income and foreign tax evidence where applicable; overseas assets and liabilities for any required disclosures.
5. Rental property, investments and other income
  • Rent agreements, property address and ownership share, actual rent received, withholding certificates and supported property expenses.
  • Property and vehicle purchase/sale papers: dates, cost/proceeds, tax receipts and payment trail.
  • Bank profit/dividend certificates, investment and brokerage statements, capital-gain/NCCPL reports where applicable, prize winnings and other income records.
  • Agricultural income and provincial tax records if relevant; gifts, inheritance or asset transfers with legal evidence and bank trail. Separate reporting or specialist review may apply.
6. Wealth statement: assets, debts and annual living costs
  • For a resident individual’s applicable wealth statement: assets and liabilities at 30 June 2026 and prior-year opening figures.
  • Property, vehicles, bank balances, cash, investments, business capital/shareholdings, jewellery and other assets, with acquisition cost and supporting history where relevant.
  • Outstanding loans and other liabilities, lender identity, balances and repayment records. Document gifts, inheritance and other sources funding asset growth.
  • Annual household spending: rent, utilities, groceries, education, medical, travel, transport and other personal costs; identify who paid shared expenses.
  • Reconcile changes in wealth to income, other supported inflows and expenses. Do not insert invented income or cash merely to balance the statement.
7. Claims, special cases and final approval
  • Zakat, eligible donations and other claimed relief: receipts and eligibility evidence. Not every donation or payment qualifies.
  • AOP/company financial statements, partner/director records, loss/depreciation schedules and applicable statutory disclosures; audited accounts where required.
  • Non-residents, foreign assets, deceased taxpayers, trusts and special tax years need a tailored checklist. Additional documents can be requested after reviewing your case.
  • Review the completed return, tax calculation and wealth reconciliation before authorizing filing. Retain submitted acknowledgments, CPRs and supporting records.

Records support preparation and verification. Exact obligations depend on your residence, income and legal form. FBR return filing guidance. Never submit CNICs, bank records, passwords or OTPs in a review.

04 · WHY FILE A TAX RETURN

The Benefits · and the Responsibilities · of Filing

Becoming a filer isn't just a legal requirement · it makes several of your financial decisions easier too.

Benefits

  • Lower withholding tax on banking, property and vehicle transactions (ATL/filer status)
  • Timely, accurate filing helps avoid applicable non-filing penalties and surcharges
  • Often required for visa applications, loan approvals and tender bids
  • Fulfils your legal obligation once income crosses the taxable threshold
  • Builds financial credibility and a documented income history

Things to Keep in Mind

  • Requires organising documents and records every year
  • Your wealth statement must be accurate and consistent year to year
  • Deadlines are strict · early preparation avoids a last-minute rush
  • Business filers need proper bookkeeping for a smooth filing
